# Running a job

Hardware: the user ran real production jobs on an ACMER 12 W on 2026-11-19 and reported that everything works; that covers Start, Pause, Resume and Frame job. The safety checklist, job size, cut check, screen wake lock, background-tab timers and Resume after an interruption (below) are built on the GRBL simulator only.

1. Connect the laser, then wait for the toolpath to be ready. If Start job is off, its tooltip says why.
2. Press **Start job** in the Job card. A safety checklist opens with four reminders (goggles, ventilation and material, fire, stay with the machine), each with a looping picture, plus the job name, layers, estimate and size. Check the material, focus and work zero, tick **I have done all of the above**, then press **Start job**. Cancel or Escape starts nothing.
3. The Job card shows Running, Finishing, then Finished, or "Stopped before the end", and Paused during a hold. STOP works throughout.

While a job runs, the Job card shows a progress bar, lines answered and elapsed time. A line counts when the laser has answered it, which is slightly ahead of the burn because GRBL buffers lines, and the elapsed time keeps counting during a pause. **Pause** holds the job, and **Resume** continues once the machine has stopped; after a safety door opened, Resume also waits until the door is closed. Pause is off when laser mode is off (`$32=0`), because the beam could stay on while the head stands still; use STOP then. The app reads `$32` when it connects, so reconnect after changing it in the console.

While a job runs, the console, jog, home, set zero, pointer and air assist are locked. After a lost connection the laser may still run what it has buffered, and the app cannot stop it, so use the machine's own power switch or emergency stop.

The Job card shows a stepper (`JobReadinessRow`, an antd `Steps` whose icons are `JobReadinessStepButton` markers; names stay under the markers, connected or not; a click on a name clicks its marker) (Profile, Artwork, Material, Check, Connect) and, under it, one button for the current required step: filled **Set up laser**, **Import SVG** or **Fix warnings (n)**, or an outlined **Connect** (plug icon), since the orange Connect on the top-bar pill is the one primary Connect. Check stays open while there is no artwork yet (it has nothing to check, so it never shows done ahead of Artwork) and while toolpath warnings lock Start job (connected or not). Every toolpath warning count (the Check badge, **Fix warnings (n)**, the Toolpath warnings chip and the object rows) comes from one selector, `selectCurrentToolpathWarnings` (`src/utils/states/toolpath.warnings.selector.ts`): a current toolpath only, and none on a new or empty project. The Cut check's own count is of cut risks, with a scissors icon, and never locks Start: its marker carries the warning count as a small badge, and **Fix warnings (n)** opens the Toolpath view and its warnings chip, and the Toolpath tab and the chip glow briefly (`flashAttentionHighlight`, `src/utils/browser/flash.attention.highlight.ts`, a `data-attention-highlight` attribute styled in `global.styles.scss`), so the view change has a cue, and the card never reads ready while Start is off. The warnings list shows each warning as its kind icon, the object name and a few words (`OBJECT_WARNING_SHORT_TEXT_KEY`), with the full sentence in a tooltip and for screen readers, and a Show button that selects the object and zooms the bed to it. An object row's chip counts the same Start-locking warnings; an object outside the bed, which does not lock Start, gets a separate bed-outline icon outside the count. The stepper looks the same in every state, with its step names; it is tight (28 px markers, no padding around the steps), and the right column's cards use tighter padding (`--panel-padding-block` on the column), so Move still fits above Stop at 1440 x 900 while connected. Material is recommended, not required (Start job does not need it): while no material is chosen its step shows a hollow, thin dotted ring with a small dashed-ring badge on the marker's corner, apart from its name, unlike the solid ring of a to-do step (the word "optional" is in its tooltip and label); each step name keeps to its own equal column at 12 px and ends in an ellipsis only when it still does not fit, and it never becomes the next step, so the stepper agrees with Start. It opens what the step opens and starts nothing: **Connect** (and the Connect step) connects in one click when a port is already chosen, sending only the same connect request as the popover's Connect; with no port chosen it opens the connection popover, which opens beside the connection pill so it never covers this button. **Import SVG** opens the same file picker as the top bar; once the artwork arrives the focus moves on to it on the bed, so the button (now the next step) is not left looking chosen. The steps are one tab stop; move between them with the arrow keys, Home and End. Filled orange is kept for this button and for Start. The chevron in the Job card heading folds the card to its facts and Start; the app remembers the fold. The card never folds by itself; when the cards below it do not fit, the right column shows a normal scrollbar and its bottom edge fades under a shadow from the Stop bar, so Move is a scroll away.

Start job and Frame job stay locked while any active content (not skipped or hidden) lies off the bed. The check (`getToolpathBedOverrun`) runs over the toolpath moves, the same points the G-code sends, so an allowed Start never moves the head past the bed (`job.start.blocked.reason.selector.test.ts` checks every origin corner). Hover the amber dot on Start to see every reason it is locked, in full, each with a warning icon; the list opens above the button, so its caption stays visible.

## Frame job

Confirmed on the ACMER 12 W by the user on 2026-11-19. The dot uses `M4`, so it goes dark when the head stands still and dims at corners.

Frame job traces the job with a dim dot, so you can see where it lands before you burn. Watch the dot to answer three questions: does the job stay within the limits of the laser, can the head travel the whole path without getting stuck, and is the sheet flat (a wider or blurrier spot shows where it rises or dips).

1. Connect the laser, wait for the toolpath to be ready and set work zero where the job should start.
2. In the Job card, choose the **Grid** (Outline only, no grid, 1 cm, 2 cm or 5 cm), a **Margin** in mm (0 by default), which grows the rectangle on all four sides, and a **Frame speed** in mm/min (6000 by default, 100 to 10000). All three are saved per machine profile. A field corrects itself only when you leave it or press Enter: left empty it gives the saved value back, and a number outside the range is held at the nearest limit. The frame time shown in the card uses the same speed, but it is a lower bound: GRBL caps every move at the machine's `$110` and `$111` limits, so a slower machine takes longer. A faster trace may also change how visible the dot is.
3. Check the dot power shown in the card: it is 0.05 W and assumes the maximum watts of the named machine profile (10 W until you change it), so set the real laser power in the profile first.
4. Press **Frame job**. There is no confirmation. The head traces the rectangle around the job, then the grid lines, switches the dot off and returns to where it started.
5. **Pause**, **Resume** and **STOP** work as for a job. The dot is off while paused. While a frame runs, the same locks as for a job apply.

Frame job is off (no fill, a solid grey outline and a dimmed icon, like Start while it cannot run; Start, Frame and Export show a lock only as the same amber dot inside the button's own corner (`ActionLockNote`, also on Start in the narrow layout's bottom bar), whose hover tooltip gives the short reason and which takes no tab stop, never an inline padlock; a ready Export is quiet, with a grey outline and muted text that brighten on hover or focus, so it never outshines Start or Frame), with the reason in its tooltip (an off Start or Frame still takes keyboard focus, so Tab reaches it, the tooltip opens on focus and a screen reader reads the reason; pressing it does nothing) (the Job card heading has no shared reason icon any more; each button's dot and description carry its own reason), and a few plain words always show right under each locked button, one reason per button ("Warnings", "Off bed", "No layer on", "Not connected" and so on; the dot shows for every locked reason, offline included, so a locked button never looks half enabled; Fix warnings no longer hides any dot, so a connected Start locked only by warnings keeps its "Warnings" dot; the Job card and the narrow bottom bar read the dot texts from one hook, `useJobLockNoteTexts`, and a reason with no short form shows its full text), so a touch screen without hover shows it too, when the job cannot start (Frame words the shared reasons for the frame, such as "Fix the toolpath warnings to trace the frame.", never "start a job"), when laser mode is not confirmed on (`$32=1`, read at connect), when there is nothing to burn, or when the frame would leave the bed. A one-line note under the job actions (`FrameBedOverrunNote`) then names the distance and the edge in a few words ("Frame +295 mm off back"), with the full sentence in its tooltip and for screen readers, and a Move job icon button (it reads the current toolpath only: once every layer is gone or off, as after New project, the background refresh regenerates an empty toolpath, so the note goes away) that selects every object in the Design view so the job can be dragged onto the bed. Work zero counts as the origin of the bed, so this check only tells you the frame fits from where work zero is; it is a first filter. The app does not know how far the head can really travel.

**The machine reports a limit only if it can.** Without soft limits (`$20=1` and a homed machine) or hard limit switches, GRBL reports nothing when the head reaches the end of its travel, and the head can stall against the frame of the machine. The app cannot detect that, so watch the head and press STOP. If the machine does report an alarm, the frame ends and the card says so; clear the alarm before framing again.

For developers: `runGrblFrame` in `src/utils/states/frame.run.saga.ts` runs the frame from the program of `src/utils/frame/`. `runGrblJob` in `src/utils/states/job.run.saga.ts` runs the job, and `selectJobStartBlockedReasonKey` holds the start conditions.

If you send `$32=` or `$RST=` in the console after connecting, Pause stays off until you reconnect, because the app reads settings only when it connects. Other ways of changing the setting, such as the machine's own screen, are not seen.

## Job size

The size of the burnable content (not the SVG canvas), as width x height in mm and cm, shows in the Design panel next to the project, in the toast after an SVG import and in the safety checklist. When both sides are within 3 mm of a DIN A size (A0 to A10, portrait or landscape) it says so with the difference, for example "419 x 594 mm, close to DIN A2 (420 x 594), 1 mm narrower"; an exact match says "DIN A2". Check it before you cut: an SVG meant as A2 can come out slightly smaller. Code: `getJobSizeMm`, `getDinAMatch`, `getJobSizeText` in `src/utils/toolpath/`, shown by `JobSizeText` (`src/views/JobSizeText/`).

## Safety checklist

Every Start job opens `JobSafetyChecklistDialog` (it replaces the old confirmation; there is no "don't show again"). Four reminders (goggles, ventilation and a safe material, fire, stay with the machine) each loop an animation while it is open (`SafetyReminderAnimation`; with reduced motion set it shows the still end frame). One checkbox, **I have done all of the above**, unlocks Start. When the laptop reports it is on battery (Battery Status API, skipped silently where unsupported) the dialog adds a hint to plug it in. Resume (below) goes through the same dialog. Frame job never does. The saga still repeats every start check, so the dialog adds a step and removes none (`job.safety.checklist.state.ts`, `JobStartRequest`).

## Long jobs

- **Screen kept awake.** While a job or frame runs the app holds a Screen Wake Lock and shows "Screen kept awake" in the Job card; it takes the lock again when the tab becomes visible (`JobWakeLock`, `screen.wake.lock.ts`). Where the browser has no Wake Lock the indicator does not show.
- **Background tabs.** Browsers slow main-thread timers in hidden tabs, so the status poller and the not-responding watchdog run on a Worker clock (`src/utils/grbl/background.timer*.ts`), which keeps ticking in a hidden tab. If the Worker cannot start or fails, they fall back to window timers (the old behaviour) and are disposed on disconnect. The stream itself stays on the main thread, driven by serial reads. Only the simulator has been tried; check a hidden tab during a long job on the real laser.
- **Leaving the page** is still guarded by `LeavePageGuard`.

## End-of-job cut check

When a job finishes normally and had a line layer, a non-blocking hint (`JobCutCheckHint`) says: don't move the sheet yet, test a part gently. Ask **Did the parts come out cleanly?**: **Yes** closes it; **Some are stuck** lists the job's line layers with a checkbox each (layers set from the Cut palette entry are ticked, others such as Score are not; fill layers are never listed) and an **Add one extra pass** option. **Run cut layers again** re-runs only the ticked layers at the same work zero, through the normal Start and checklist, so do not move the sheet or the work zero. Nothing is forced: ignore or close the hint at any time.

Material note: each layer remembers the preset it was last set from (`presetId`; old projects load without it, and editing power, speed or passes by hand clears the link). Once you answer **Some are stuck**, the presets of the ticked linked layers get a dismissible note ("did not cut through last time, consider an extra pass or more power"), shown in the material library and when the preset is applied. The note is added when you press Run cut layers again, or when you close the hint after answering. Notes are never applied to the layer settings; pressing **Update** on a preset (new values) drops its note. The hint stays after a re-cut, which can fail to cut through too. Code: `getRecutToolpath`, `job.cut.check.selector.ts`, `material.preset.state.ts`.

## Interrupted job and Resume

After a stop, reset, alarm or lost connection the Job card shows where the job stopped: the layer, the percentage and the last confirmed point (`JobInterruptionSummary`). In the same session, **Resume** (`JobResumeControls`) can continue it:

1. If the machine profile has homing switches, Resume homes first (`$H`), so the head finds work zero again (Set zero is kept in G54). Without homing the card warns that the head must be at exactly the same work zero as before, and suggests Frame job first. The start position is read before homing.
2. It backs up 20 G-code lines from the last answered one (`RESUME_BACKUP_LINE_COUNT`), because GRBL buffers lines ahead of the burn: a short double burn is preferred to a gap.
3. A header restores laser mode with power zero (`M4 S0`), then the head travels to that move with the laser off, then the program continues with its own modal state (`getResumeToolpath`, `getResumeMoveIndex`).
4. Resume goes through the safety checklist. The card suggests Frame job first and not to touch the material.

Resume is refused when the project or the machine profile changed since the stop, or when Start is blocked for another reason (the reason shows in the card). A reload or a new session loses the resume point. Live feed and power overrides were dropped: the user does not need them.

## Stable layout while the machine works

Nothing in the Job card changes size when the machine turns busy, a run starts or ends, or an action locks:

- A locked Start, Frame or Export shows why as a small amber dot on the top corner of its button (`ActionLockNote`: an antd warning-status `Badge` under a hover-only antd `Popover`); hover, tap or focus it for the reason (for example "Busy" while the laser moves). The dot floats over the button, so it never pushes the buttons down. It shows whenever the button is `aria-disabled`, while Fix warnings shows too, on desktop and in the narrow bottom bar alike (`useJobLockNoteTexts`).
- The run status (Running, Paused, Finished...) is a blue dot with the same tooltip, in a slot that is always reserved in the facts line; screen readers hear it from a hidden live region.
- The header keeps a slot for the reasons icon, so the fold button never moves.

## Return to the start position

When a job finishes normally, the laser goes back to where it was when you pressed Start: the beam is switched off (`M5`), then one absolute jog in work coordinates (`$J=G90 G21 X.. Y.. F<jog feed>`) takes the head there (`job.start.position.return.saga.ts`). After a stop, a reset, an alarm or an error the laser stays where it ended. Frame runs are not affected.
